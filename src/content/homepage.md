# Things We've Actually Tried

## Questions are more useful after they meet reality.

We like thought experiments.

We like them much more once something has been built.

### Making a local canteen easier to find

`BUILT · LOCAL COMMERCE`

Accent On Health is an office canteen whose physical location made it easy for potential customers on other floors to miss.

We created a lightweight digital menu with direct WhatsApp ordering and placed QR-based discovery near higher-footfall areas such as office coffee machines.

The experiment was deliberately simple:

**make an existing local business easier to discover without inserting a marketplace between the customer and vendor.**

What began as a small practical intervention later helped shape our broader questions around local-commerce discoverability.

**Related idea: Commerce & Livelihoods →**

### Turning job-search friction into a learning signal

`PROTOTYPE · LEARNING`

Career OS began from another ordinary problem:

job applications generate a great deal of information, but very little of it returns to the candidate as structured learning.

The prototype looks across opportunities and recurring requirements to distinguish between:

**a capability that exists but is poorly represented**

and

**a capability that genuinely needs to be developed.**

Repeated mismatch can then become input to a learning plan rather than disappearing into application history.

**Related idea: Learning & Capability →**

### Giving an old phone another job

`RUNNING EXPERIMENT · AI INFRASTRUCTURE`

A retired Android phone does not necessarily need to become electronic waste simply because it is no longer a good primary phone.

We repurposed one as a small always-on computing node running automation and remote-access services through tools such as Termux, n8n, Tailscale and Cloudflare Tunnel.

Other experiments have explored exposing local AI runtime inside a trusted private network.

The larger question:

**How much useful infrastructure can we extract from computing capacity people already own?**

**Related idea: AI Within Reach →**

# Build · Explore · Enable

## How we approach problems

We are not trying to predict which idea must become a company.

The process matters more.

### Build

**Make the question touch reality.**

Create the smallest useful experiment.

Use existing technology where it works.

Build new machinery only where the problem actually demands it.

A prototype should teach us something that discussion alone cannot.

### Explore

**Follow the problem beyond the first implementation.**

A simple storefront can expose a question about economic discoverability.

A learning workflow can expose a prerequisite-graph problem.

A personal automation can expose questions about authority and responsibility.

We use prototypes to discover the deeper systems problem underneath them.

### Enable

**Make useful capability easier to reach.**

Sometimes technology should replace an inefficient process.

Often it can instead help existing people and infrastructure do more.

A shopkeeper.

A teacher.

A cobbler.

A local fabrication studio.

A commuter.

A device someone already owns.

We are interested in systems that connect those capabilities rather than assuming everything valuable must first be centralized.
